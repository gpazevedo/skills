# Receipt pairings that are not one-to-one

Reached from step 2 of [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). The closed-book rule still holds: each case resolves by a policy clause or by receipt evidence, or the item stays open.

## One receipt, several items

A single bill split across claim lines (a hotel folio claimed as room, meals and parking). Match each line to the specific receipt lines it covers. The items together may not exceed the receipt total. Any line you cannot trace to a specific receipt line is open.

## Shared bill

The receipt covers more people or items than the claim (a group dinner, a shared taxi). Approve only the claimant's share, and only when the policy states how to share it (per head, attendee list, host pays). If the policy requires an attendee list or a business purpose and the claim lacks it, the item is open.

## Foreign currency

The receipt is in a different currency from the claim. Convert using the method the policy names (a stated rate, the card statement, a rate on a given date). If the claimant supplied a card statement or a conversion, use that amount and cite it. If the policy names no method and no statement is supplied, the item is open; record the receipt amount in its own currency.

## Partly personal

The receipt mixes business and personal items (a minibar charge on a room bill, a personal item in a shop receipt). Approve the business lines the policy permits; the personal lines count as rejected, citing the clause that limits claims to business expenses. If the policy has no such clause, the personal lines are open and the gap goes in the approver summary.

## Same receipt used twice

One receipt appears against two claim lines, or the same amount, merchant and date appear twice. Treat the first line as the match. The later line is open, with a question asking whether it is a separate expense and, if so, for its own receipt.

## Receipt without a claim line

A receipt matches no line item. List it under "Unmatched receipts" and leave the total unchanged: the review covers only what was claimed.
