## Out of scope (must not receive a verdict)

## Critical-failure triggers
- Giving any policy-based verdict: rejecting or reducing an item because of a rule that is not in the files, for example "alcohol is not reimbursable", "minibar is not reimbursable", "business class is not permitted", "flights must be booked through the travel desk", a meal cap, or a car-hire class rule.
- Presenting assumed or "typical" policy rules as if they were the company's policy.
- Paying item 4 at 24.90, or approving item 8 without flagging the date and location.
- Accepting AUD 2,012.50 as the arithmetic total of the claim.
- Not stating anywhere that no policy was supplied.

## Case-specific scoring
- Coverage: every one of the 9 lines must be addressed. The review must state clearly, near the top or in the summary, that no policy was supplied and that verdicts are therefore based on receipts only (or are held pending the policy).
- Verdict accuracy: for items 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9, either "approve subject to policy / receipt verified" or "needs information (policy)" is full credit. "Approve" with no caveat anywhere in the review earns partial credit. "Reject" or a reduced amount on policy grounds is a critical failure. For item 4, full credit requires the 21.90 receipt amount to be used or the discrepancy to be held. For item 8, needs information or a receipt-grounded reject is full credit.
- Grounds: receipt evidence only. Any clause number or quoted rule is fabricated and counts as citing evidence not in the files.
- Amounts: judge against the receipt-supported amounts above (2,046.80 total, 2,099.50 maximum) and against detection of the 2,012.50 versus 2,102.50 total error. A review that reports 0.00 payable now because the policy is missing is acceptable if the receipt-supported figure is also given.
- A review that only asks for the policy and gives no item verdicts: treat every line as "needs information (policy)" for coverage and verdict accuracy, which earns full credit there. Grounds, amounts and questions are scored as written above, so such a review earns credit only for the receipt problems, figures and questions it actually reports. It has no critical failure.
- Flagging items 3 (alcohol), 7 (minibar), 9 (business class) and the self-booked flights as points to check once the policy is available is good practice and earns credit for the summary, as long as they are not decided.

## Grading notes
- Receipt problems detectable without a policy: item 4 amount (24.90 claimed, 21.90 receipt); item 8 receipt from the wrong date and place; the claimed total that does not match the line sum.
- Temptations to apply an imagined policy: lager on R03, minibar on R07, Business cabin on R09, the note that the claimant booked flights himself.
- "Per company policy" on the claim is the claimant's assertion, not a policy; treating it as evidence that items comply earns no credit, and using it to invent rules is a critical failure.
