name: meeting-notes-to-decision-brief
description: Turn meeting notes into a decision brief. Use when the user supplies meeting notes, a transcript, or minutes and wants decisions, action items, or a summary.
---

Produce a decision brief from the notes the user supplied. The notes are the only source: every line of the brief traces to a quoted or closely paraphrased passage.

## Classify first

Before writing, go through the notes passage by passage and label each substantive statement with one status:

- **Decision**: the notes show commitment, such as "agreed", "approved", "we will", or no objection after a call for it. Cite the passage.
- **Proposal**: suggested, floated, or favoured but with no commitment shown. "We should", "I suggest", "leaning towards" all land here, whatever the speaker's seniority.
- **Open**: raised and left unanswered, deferred, or lacking a needed fact (owner, deadline, rationale, number).
- **Conflict**: two passages disagree, within the notes or between speakers. Keep both sides.

When a statement's status is ambiguous, file it as Proposal and say what evidence would make it a Decision. Done when every substantive statement carries exactly one label.

## Write the brief

Use these sections, in this order. Omit nothing; write "None found in the notes." for an empty section.

1. **Decisions**: one entry each, with the decision in a sentence, then the supporting evidence as a short quote or close paraphrase from the notes, with who said it if the notes say.
2. **Proposals not yet decided**: each with its proposer if stated and what is needed to settle it.
3. **Action items**: a table of action, owner, deadline. Copy owners and deadlines exactly as the notes state them, including relative ones ("next Friday"). Where either is absent, write `Not stated`; an action without a stated owner stays unowned.
4. **Open questions and contradictions**: each open question with what is missing; each contradiction with both passages quoted side by side.
5. **Handoff summary**: at most five sentences for a reader who skipped the meeting: what was decided, what is owed and by whom, what is still unresolved.

## Unresolved stays unresolved

Missing information is a finding, reported as missing. Leave dates, owners, figures, names, and rationales that the notes do not give as `Not stated`, and never infer them from context, role, or typical practice. Resolve a conflict only when the notes themselves resolve it. If the handoff summary cannot say something without a guess, it says the item is unresolved.

## Check before returning

Confirm each item against the notes:

- Every Decision has a cited passage.
- Every owner and deadline appears verbatim in the notes, or reads `Not stated`.
- Every Proposal, Open, and Conflict item from the classification appears in the brief.
- The handoff summary matches the sections above it.
